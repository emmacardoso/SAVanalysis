## Investigating the Relationship Between Water Quality Variables and NDAVI at North Campus Open Space in Goleta, CA.

## Overview
This ongoing personal project analyzes the relationship between water quality metrics and the abundance of submerged aquatic vegetation at North Campus Open Space (NCOS) in Goleta, California. Beginning in 2013, the Cheadle Center for Biodiversity and Ecological Restoration restored NCOS from a golf course to a its native wetland habitat. Water quality data was collected from three sites throughout North Campus Open Space's Devereux Slough, as well as from Devereux Lagoon, a connected region of the wetland belonging to Coal Oil Point Reserve (COPR) that has been restored for a longer period of time. The project aims to assess how water quality variables influence submerged vegetation abundance. Additionally, this analysis explores if there are differences in the newly restored NCOS wetland and the COPR region.

## Data
Water Quality data includes dissolved oxygen saturation, dissolved oxygen mg/L, conductivity, salinity, temperature, and pH. Water quality data was collected using a YSI instrument, pH meter, and data from the Cheadle Center for Biodiversity and Ecological Restoration. To determine the abundance of submerged aquatic vegetation, NDAVI was calculated from remotely-sensed data and satelitte imagery from Planet Labs.
